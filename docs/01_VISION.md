# Game vision and experience

**Status:** Shared design vision, 2026-11-13. Specific mechanics remain experimental.

## Elevator pitch

**Addiction²** (working title) is an original survival action RPG combining **Valheim-like exploration, survival, freedom, and base building** with **Metin2-like class/character progression, memorable gear, equipment enhancement, quests, dungeons, bosses, and satisfying repeatable farming**. A light New World-style profession progression layer rewards mining, woodcutting, and farming.

A world where you can **live** for hundreds of hours meets a character/equipment loop you can **progress** for hundreds of hours. The intended combination was jokingly called **“addiction²”** — the goal is enjoyable, voluntary, rewarding play, **not** coercive engagement design.

## Priority of inspirations

1. **Valheim = foundation:** home construction, survival, food/preparation, exploring dangerous regions, resources, forests, sailing, co-op, the ability to take your time and not rush bosses.
2. **Metin2 = missing complementary depth:** recognisable starting-town/NPC structure (Map1 feel), classes and combat skills, character levels, monster farming, rare memorable weapons, +0 to +9 enhancement and switchable bonuses, quests, story, dungeons/bosses.
3. **New World = limited enhancement:** make mining, woodcutting, and farming satisfying professions with visible progression; not a 40-skill MMO profession system.
4. Elden Ring and Diablo 4 were **examples** showing that RPG levels/classes work outside MMOs; neither is a fourth equal pillar. Rust and 7 Days to Die were comparisons for mining feel, not commitments to their systems.

## Player freedom

- Play single-player **or** with friends.
- Spend tonight building, farming, sailing, collecting resources, exploring, grinding monsters, running dungeons, or tweaking equipment; no activity must be the “correct” way to enjoy the game.
- Maintain your own home outside the starting town. The starting settlement is an **early-game base** and service hub, not the only place to live.
- No daily checklist, weekly reset anxiety, battle-pass pressure, or competitive MMO economy/leaderboard obligations.
- Gear-grind targets may take many sessions; occasional partial progress should still feel meaningful.
- The activities must be fun in themselves, not merely gates before a reward.
- You can challenge dangerous content while “underleveled.” Preparation, buffs, good food, friends, item investment, and personal skill can overcome enemies intended for stronger characters.
- Combat classes shape fighting but must **never** lock building, mining, farming, exploration, or basic survival gameplay. Every class must be capable of finishing the main game alone; efficiency can differ.
- Co-op should create “bro, help me farm this dungeon earlier!” moments, not mandatory healer/tank/team-composition checks.
- Friend groups may choose how character XP is shared and can distribute effort between combat, home construction, and resource preparation.

## Key fantasy: the town and home

You arrive at a **Map1-inspired** medieval starting town (Metin2 town layout/recognisable NPC roles, but with Valheim's architecture, atmosphere, and immersion). The town is initially your safe base: meet the blacksmith, merchant/elder/hunter-type NPCs as needed, prepare for adventures and quests. **No player building inside the town.** Later you establish a home in the wilderness and keep returning to the town only when its services/story unlocks matter. Other settlements and ships may expand the world later but are not yet specified.

## Key fantasy: the builder and the adventurer

One friend is primarily a warrior who farms monsters and investigates dungeons. Another enjoys constructing and maintaining the shared base, growing food, and gathering; they **may** be a support-oriented shaman because they enjoy helping on adventures, but that is their choice. They can share **character XP** from the combat-focused player even while far apart. Their professions still advance from performing their own gathering activities. They team up to attempt higher-danger content **early** because preparation, buffs, teamwork, and skill create opportunities. Then each returns to their preferred activity.

## Key fantasy: the cherished weapon

A player finds an identifiable, desirable weapon, sees an exceptional item-specific random roll (possibly DSS/FKS for **one special weapon**), upgrades it at the blacksmith, adjusts eligible regular bonuses, and proudly keeps using it for dozens of hours. Most normal equipment can have predictable base performance and shared regular bonus pools. **We are not making every weapon a randomized DSS/FKS weapon.**

## Unifying design principle

**“Class determines your combat identity, not how you're allowed to enjoy the survival world.”**

**“Higher level makes content easier; it must not be the only way to overcome difficulty.”**

**“Your base provides independence. Towns provide opportunities.”** This last one is a suggested guiding principle, not a confirmed rule requiring exclusive town-based repairs or services.

## What success looks like in the prototype

The control and camera feel good first. Later, combat and loot are enjoyable enough that the player voluntarily wants another encounter or another chance at their desired sword. Still later, the base-building/survival activity and RPG grind reinforce each other instead of interrupting each other. Test, do not merely assume.
